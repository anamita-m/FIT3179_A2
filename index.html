How Australia Voted in 2025

The Labor–Coalition duopoly is shrinking. Teal independents, the Greens and One Nation are taking a bigger share of the vote. Younger Australians are leaving the Coalition for Labor and the Greens, and women are less likely than men to vote Coalition and more likely to vote Greens or Labor.

Voter demographics

Income, age and gender are splitting the vote in ways that cut across the old party lines.

Three factors shape how Australians vote: income, generation and gender.

Income
  • Higher-income suburbs lean towards teal independents and Labor.
  • Lower-income areas lean more towards the Coalition.
  • Minor parties win far more support in lower-income areas.
Generation
  • Younger Australians are leaving the Coalition for Labor and the Greens.
  • Labor and the Greens gain much of the support the Coalition loses.
  • Gen Z results rest on only 83 survey respondents, so read them with care.
Gender
  • Men are more likely to vote Coalition, by 8.2 percentage points.
  • Women are more likely to vote Labor, by 5.3 points.
  • Women are more likely to vote Greens, by 9.9 points.

Voting turnout and informal voting

Voting is compulsory, but not every ballot counts. Where do votes go missing?

Turnout is the share of enrolled voters who cast a ballot. In 2025, national turnout was 90.67%, the second-lowest in a century, even though enrolment reached a record 98.2% of eligible Australians (18,098,797 people).

Informal votes are ballots that cannot be counted. A vote is informal when:

  • it is blank or unmarked;
  • ticks or crosses are used where numbers in order of preference are required;
  • the required number of boxes has not been marked; or
  • it contains writing that identifies the voter.

The national informality rate in 2025 was 5.6%. The Australian Electoral Commission (AEC) noted the highest rates in Western Sydney electorates, often linked to lower English proficiency, and in Riverina and Calwell, where an unusually large number of candidates appeared on the ballot paper.
